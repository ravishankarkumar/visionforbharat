I believe India’s reservation system will undergo a substantial reorganisation around 2050. This is a forecast, not a demand for its removal and not a claim that its constitutional purpose will disappear.

The commitment to correcting historical exclusion may endure even if the machinery used to pursue it changes. Categories, evidence, objectives, duration, and methods of implementation may all be reconsidered as India itself changes.

“Reorganisation” therefore does not automatically mean abolition, expansion, or any other predetermined outcome. The more useful question is what inequalities will exist around 2050, how they should be measured, and which institutions can respond to them fairly without forgetting the history that produced them.

This note records the forecast early. A fuller essay will need to examine why 2050 may become a transition point, which principles must survive any reorganisation, and what evidence would prove this expectation wrong.
